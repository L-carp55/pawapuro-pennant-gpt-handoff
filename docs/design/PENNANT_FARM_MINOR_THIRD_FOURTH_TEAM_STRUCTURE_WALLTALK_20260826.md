# Pennant farm / minor / third-fourth team competition structure wall-talk — 2026-08-26

Status: **OWNER WALL-TALK PRESERVED / CURRENT-NPB STRUCTURE RESEARCH ANCHORED / IMPLEMENTATION NOT AUTHORIZED**

This document preserves wall-talk for OD-08 `farm_minor_third_fourth_team_competition_structure`. It supplements development, roster rights, injury/rehab, organizational innovation, finance, scheduling, global-world and history design. It creates no new canonical PW IDs.

## 1. Core principle: an extra farm team is not a flat development buff

Do not model a third/fourth team as:

`3rd team exists -> all prospects +X% growth`.

The value of additional development teams must come from real mechanisms such as:

- more actual plate appearances / innings / fielding repetitions;
- better separation by current level and role;
- more opportunities for position conversion / pitch development / mechanics experiments;
- dedicated rehabilitation competition;
- more chances for late-blooming or low-draft-status players to remain in the organization;
- increased scouting/evaluation evidence on internal players.

If a club lacks players, coaches, facilities or opponents, an extra team may provide little benefit and can even dilute resources.

## 2. Distinguish organizational team level from competition circuit

A club's internal hierarchy and the league/circuit it plays in are separate concepts.

Possible organizational units:

- first team;
- official farm/second team;
- third team;
- fourth team;
- rookie/development squad;
- rehab group;
- academy team.

Possible competition types:

- official professional farm league;
- developmental league;
- independent-league opponents;
- university/industrial-team games where institutionally plausible;
- other organizations' third/fourth teams;
- international/winter/development competition;
- exhibitions / practice games;
- internal scrimmages.

Do not assume every organizational level has its own official league.

## 3. Current 2026 NPB farm structure is an initial real-world anchor

Official NPB information states that from 2026 the farm competition uses a one-league, three-district structure:

- East district: 5 clubs;
- Central district: 5 clubs;
- West district: 4 clubs;
- district play plus cross-district games;
- scheduled game totals differ by club;
- the current farm universe includes Oisix Niigata and Hayate Shizuoka as clubs in the farm competition.

This is current-world rule/structure data, not a permanent architecture assumption.

The simulation must allow this structure to evolve in future decades.

## 4. Farm-only / non-top-league organizations are first-class entities

Oisix/Hayate-style organizations demonstrate that a club can participate in a development/farm competition without being a normal first-division NPB franchise.

The world model should therefore support organizations that are:

- full top-league clubs;
- lower/farm-only professional clubs;
- independent-league clubs;
- affiliates/development partners;
- academy/development organizations.

Do not model every opponent as merely a hidden farm squad belonging to one of the 12 top clubs.

## 5. Third/fourth teams can schedule outside the official farm league

A third/fourth team need not require NPB to create a new official league immediately.

It can obtain games through:

- other organizations' development teams;
- independent clubs;
- university/industrial teams where rules allow;
- exhibitions;
- regional development circuits;
- overseas development trips;
- internal competition.

The quality, frequency and travel cost of those games determine actual value.

## 6. Real playing time is the scarce resource

Development value should depend on actual usage rather than the nominal number of teams.

Track conceptually:

- plate appearances;
- batters faced / innings;
- defensive innings / repetitions;
- baserunning opportunities;
- role-specific usage;
- practice and mechanics work;
- opponent quality;
- rest/recovery.

A prospect assigned to a third team but barely playing does not gain a magical benefit from the team label.

## 7. Playing time has diminishing returns and opportunity cost

Existing PW direction remains:

- more real experience can help;
- first-team challenge is not inherently a development penalty;
- bench inactivity can be worse than regular farm play;
- experience has diminishing returns;
- development is not proportional forever to raw game count.

A farm system must allocate opportunity rather than simply maximize total games without limit.

## 8. Competition level affects learning context, not an RPG recommended-level penalty

Opponent strength matters because it changes:

- what skills are tested;
- success/failure information;
- tactical complexity;
- physical/mental demands;
- quality of repetitions.

Do not use a rule such as `player rating below league rating -> development -50%`.

A player can learn while struggling against stronger competition, but the club still pays the competitive/opportunity cost of using him there.

## 9. Development objectives affect assignment

The best assignment is not always the highest possible team.

A club may choose between:

- first-team bench;
- everyday official-farm role;
- specialized third-team role;
- rehab competition;
- position-conversion workload;
- mechanics/pitch-development program.

The choice depends on the player's current needs, available playing time and club objectives.

## 10. Player assignment is constrained by roster-rights rules

The farm system cannot ignore the Roster Rights Engine.

Assignment legality depends on league-specific statuses such as:

- NPB controlled/development registration;
- active registration/cooldown;
- MLB 40-man/options/waivers;
- injury/rehab eligibility;
- contract/consent rights;
- foreign/domestic classification where relevant.

Organizational desire and institutional permission remain separate.

## 11. Third/fourth teams increase capacity but also consume resources

Costs can include:

- coaches;
- medical/training staff;
- analysts;
- facilities;
- travel;
- opponent scheduling;
- equipment/logistics;
- housing/support;
- larger player payroll/pool;
- management attention.

This connects directly to PW-150 and the finance system.

## 12. Coaching resources can dilute

If a club expands from two teams to four without adding enough qualified staff, player-to-coach attention can worsen.

More teams therefore create a tradeoff between:

- more opportunities;
- deeper specialization;
- thinner expertise/attention;
- higher financial cost.

Do not assume big-budget clubs can buy infinite development simply by adding teams.

## 13. Opponent availability is a real constraint

A fourth team cannot play 140 high-quality games if no suitable opponents exist.

Game supply depends on:

- regional baseball ecosystem;
- other pro organizations;
- independent/university/industrial relationships;
- travel budget;
- league rules;
- facilities;
- calendar compatibility.

This creates value for network capital and long-term pipeline building.

## 14. Network capital can create better development schedules

Existing international/organizational network systems can help clubs secure:

- recurring opponents;
- winter-league slots;
- exchange series;
- overseas camps;
- developmental tournaments.

The advantage comes from relationship infrastructure and opportunities, not a permanent hidden team buff.

## 15. Internal team identities can differ by purpose

A club may assign different mandates, for example:

- second team: near-first-team readiness and official farm competition;
- third team: younger/projection players, controlled experiments, depth;
- fourth team: raw/rehab/low-level development, academy transition.

These are organizational policies, not fixed universal definitions.

Another club may use a different hierarchy.

## 16. Promotion/demotion between development teams is continuous and purpose-driven

Movement can occur because of:

- performance/readiness;
- first-team need;
- injury replacement;
- playing-time bottleneck;
- developmental objective;
- position/pitch/mechanics experiment;
- rehab progression;
- roster-rights requirement.

Do not automatically promote after a fixed number of successful games.

## 17. Rehab competition is a special objective

A rehabbing player may use lower-level games because they provide controlled workload and timing, not because the player has become a low-level talent.

The medical system should set permitted workload/readiness; the farm system supplies appropriate competitive opportunities.

## 18. Position conversion and mechanics development need game opportunities

A player learning catcher, shortstop, a new pitching role or new mechanics requires actual repetitions in addition to practice.

Third/fourth teams can create low-cost experimental opportunities, but:

- mistakes still occur;
- player health/workload matters;
- coaching quality matters;
- competition quality affects evidence.

No guaranteed conversion simply because a lower team exists.

## 19. Farm stats are observations, not direct growth currency

Second/third/fourth team statistics can help evaluate:

- current performance;
- role readiness;
- adaptation;
- mechanics change;
- trait evidence.

But `batting .350 -> automatic contact +5` remains prohibited.

The truth/evaluation/performance separation applies at every level.

## 20. Official and unofficial statistics must be distinguished

Store the context of each game:

- official league game;
- official lower-level/development league;
- exhibition;
- practice game;
- international/winter league;
- rehab appearance.

Career-history UI can summarize appropriately without mixing every exhibition into official statistical records.

## 21. Farm championships/titles can matter without becoming the main objective

Development teams may pursue competitive success because:

- winning has organizational/fan value;
- players/staff care;
- official titles/history matter.

But the parent organization can rationally prioritize player development/experimentation over maximizing every farm-game win.

This is not equivalent to a top-level CPU club intentionally refusing to compete for championships.

## 22. Development-team manager objectives differ from top-team objectives

A farm manager may balance:

- winning today's game;
- mandated playing time;
- position conversion;
- pitch limits;
- rehab workload;
- prospect priority;
- first-team readiness.

The parent organization sets policy; farm staff execute within those goals.

## 23. Players can be blocked even in a deep organization

A 3rd/4th-team system reduces but does not eliminate playing-time scarcity.

A club can still accumulate too many players at the same position/role, creating:

- reduced repetitions;
- dissatisfaction;
- trade/Active Draft/release pressure;
- loan/overseas/development opportunities where rules allow.

Do not let larger farm systems erase roster-market pressure entirely.

## 24. Multi-team systems can improve internal evaluation

More games can provide more observations for scouts/coaches/analysts about:

- actual player skill;
- role fit;
- injury recovery;
- special-trait evidence;
- mechanics stability.

But more observations do not reveal latent truth perfectly.

## 25. Development quality depends on player fit, not one facility level

A player can benefit differently from the same farm system depending on:

- current skill needs;
- role;
- learning response;
- coach fit;
- language/culture adaptation;
- health;
- playing-time availability;
- competition level.

Avoid a universal `farm quality = 92 -> growth x1.25` multiplier.

## 26. CPU clubs can create, expand, repurpose or shrink extra teams

A third/fourth team is an organizational innovation choice.

CPU/user clubs should consider:

- size of player pipeline;
- budget;
- available staff;
- facilities;
- opponent network;
- current bottlenecks;
- strategic philosophy;
- evidence that the system is producing value.

A club can later reduce or close a level if costs exceed benefits.

## 27. Existing real-world advantages are initial assets, not eternal club traits

If a 2026 club already has a sophisticated multi-team system, preserve that as an initial organizational state.

Over decades:

- rivals can imitate;
- staff can be poached;
- methods can diffuse;
- the origin club can improve or decline;
- league institutions can change.

No permanent `SoftBank development bonus` or equivalent.

## 28. League-level farm reform is possible

Over 100 years, league governance may change:

- number of farm clubs;
- district structure;
- official affiliation rules;
- admission of independent/farm-only organizations;
- playoff/championship format;
- roster eligibility;
- geographic grouping;
- game counts.

The 2026 one-league/three-district structure is not frozen forever.

## 29. Expansion/reorganization must update the farm system too

If the top league expands from 12 to 14/16 clubs, the game must decide how development competition adapts:

- new farm teams;
- district realignment;
- affiliate agreements;
- schedule regeneration;
- facility/opponent requirements.

Do not expand only the top league while leaving an impossible old farm schedule.

## 30. Global leagues use different development structures

MLB affiliates, NPB organization-owned teams, KBO/CPBL structures, independent systems and future leagues may use different hierarchies.

The engine should share primitives such as:

- organizational affiliation;
- competition level;
- roster rights;
- assignment;
- schedule;
- playing time;
- staff/resources.

Do not rename every global level `2軍/3軍` internally or in UI.

## 31. Farm-only clubs can have their own economic and sporting goals

A non-top-level professional club should be able to:

- develop players;
- win its competition;
- sign/release players under its rules;
- build local popularity;
- serve as a destination after NPB release;
- become a scouting source for top clubs.

It is not merely a neutral sparring opponent generator.

## 32. Player movement from independent/farm-only clubs remains a market transaction

If an NPB/top-league club wants such a player, acquisition should respect:

- contract rights;
- transfer rules;
- scouting/evaluation;
- player preference;
- competing clubs;
- deadlines.

Do not spawn the player into a free candidate list detached from his existing club/history.

## 33. User-facing management remains light

The user should usually control:

- organizational development policy;
- creation/closure of major team levels;
- selected priority prospects;
- important assignments/conversions/rehab decisions;
- staff/resource investment.

Routine lineups and daily assignments can be delegated.

Do not require manual management of four full lineups every day unless the user explicitly chooses that depth.

## 34. AI should surface playing-time bottlenecks

Useful staff reports include:

- `遊撃手候補が多く、3名の実戦機会が不足しています`;
- `この投手は二軍先発枠が埋まっており、三軍での先発機会の方が多くなります`;
- `リハビリ登板に適した試合が来週あります`;
- `四軍は対戦相手不足で予定試合数を確保できていません`.

This makes the underlying depth actionable without micromanagement.

## 35. Schedule quality is part of development infrastructure

A club with many teams but poor scheduling can still fail to provide useful repetitions.

Evaluate:

- games played;
- cancellations;
- opponent quality mix;
- travel burden;
- rest;
- positional opportunity;
- pitching workload availability.

This connects OD-08 directly to the future schedule/weather/travel engine.

## 36. Weather/cancellation can matter more in development circuits

Current 2026 NPB farm rules note that canceled farm games are not necessarily rescheduled under the current format.

The simulation should therefore preserve the idea that development opportunities can be lost to calendar/weather constraints depending on the rule pack.

Do not guarantee a fixed number of farm repetitions regardless of actual schedule outcomes.

## 37. More games are not always better

Excessive schedule volume can create:

- fatigue;
- injury risk;
- reduced training time;
- travel cost;
- thin pitching availability;
- lower competition quality.

Organizations must balance games, practice and recovery.

## 38. Long-run development equilibrium needs explicit QA

Track across 20/50/100-year saves:

- plate appearances/innings by age and level;
- percentage of players with inadequate opportunity;
- team-level roster congestion;
- third/fourth-team game completion;
- opponent availability;
- staff-to-player ratios;
- farm-system costs;
- promotion rates;
- development outcomes after controlling for player quality;
- whether richer clubs obtain automatic permanent dominance;
- whether extra-team systems diffuse realistically.

## 39. QA must detect fake multi-team value

Test that:

- adding a third team without extra players/opponents/coaches does not magically improve everyone;
- added real playing time can improve development opportunities;
- over-expansion can dilute coaching and finances;
- a two-team organization with excellent opportunity allocation can outperform a badly managed four-team organization;
- roster-rights restrictions are respected at every level;
- rehab and conversion assignments use the correct objectives;
- CPU clubs actively solve playing-time bottlenecks rather than leaving prospects idle for years.

## 40. Guardrails

- No flat third-team/fourth-team development buff.
- No guaranteed playing time merely because a team level exists.
- No universal recommended-level development penalty.
- No farm batting/pitching stats as direct ability-growth currency.
- No assumption that every organizational level has an official league.
- No free infinite opponent supply.
- No free infinite coaching attention.
- No multi-team system that erases roster/market scarcity.
- No permanent club-exclusive development buff from a 2026 organizational advantage.
- No requirement that the user manually manage every lower-team lineup.
- No implementation from this document yet.
